Last weekend I made Moth Lamp for an online jam with the theme "drawn to the light". You are a moth. You hold one button to fly towards the lamp and let go to drift away before you burn. That is the whole game.

The jam ran for {hours} hours, which is {hours * 60} minutes. It felt like much less.

## Planning

### One verb

I wrote one rule on a sticky note before I opened the editor: one button, no menus. Every idea after that got checked against the note. Most did not survive, which was the point.

### The time budget

| Block      | Hours | What happened                      |
| :--------- | ----: | :--------------------------------- |
| Prototype  |     6 | Moth movement and the heat meter   |
| Art        |    14 | Sprites, the lamp, the background  |
| Sound      |     4 | Three sound effects and a hum      |
| Polish     |    10 | Screen shake, a title, a high score |
| Sleep      |    14 | Not negotiable                     |

## Building it

### The heat meter

The core is a number that rises near the lamp and falls away from it:

```ts
const heat = Math.max(0, heatPrev + (1 / (dist * dist + 1)) * 40 - 0.6 * dt);
if (heat > 1) burn();
```

### Sprites

The moth has {frames} frames in total: four directions with six frames of wing flaps each. I drew them at 16 by 16 pixels in a 16 colour palette.

#### Wing flaps

Six frames was too many. In play nobody can see the difference between six and four, and four would have saved me two hours.

> [!NOTE]
> The canvas is drawn at 160 by 120 and scaled up with CSS. Pixel art needs `image-rendering: pixelated` on the canvas, or the browser blurs it.

## What I would do differently

- Add sound in the first six hours. Sound made the game feel finished more than any of the art did.
- Test on a phone on the first day. The one-button idea was perfect for touch, and I found out on the last evening.

> [!TIP]
> Submit a working build twelve hours before the deadline, then keep improving it. Upload servers get slow in the last hour.
