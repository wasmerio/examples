For a long time my mornings were an attempt to get ahead. Up at six, at the desk by half past, email cleared before the rest of the house woke. It worked in the sense that a lot got done. It failed in the sense that by ten I was already tired in a way that no amount of coffee touched, and the work I did in that first hour was rarely the work that mattered.

## What changed

The change was not a decision so much as an injury. A wrist problem kept me away from the keyboard for six weeks last winter, and the first hour of the day suddenly had nothing in it. I made coffee. I sat by the window. I read a few pages of whatever was on the table. By the second week I noticed that the day went better, not despite the empty hour but because of it.

When the wrist healed I kept the hour empty. This is roughly what it looks like now:

- Kettle on, and a short walk to the end of the street while it boils. In winter this is the only daylight I get before noon.
- Coffee, made slowly, drunk at the table and not at the desk.
- Twenty pages of a book, on paper, with a pencil for the margins.
- A single line in the notebook about the day ahead: not a plan, just the one thing that matters.
- Then, and only then, the desk.

No phone until the desk. That rule does most of the work.[^1]

## The objection

The obvious objection is that an hour is a lot. I have a job, and the people I edit for do not care how rested I am. But I have not lost the hour. It comes back in the afternoon, when I used to stall for exactly that long, staring at a paragraph and calling it thinking.

A line from a book I keep on the desk explains this better than I can:

> Attention is a finite thing, and it is spent long before you notice the account is empty.

I used to spend it on the inbox before anyone else was awake. Now the first hour goes to the things that give it back: light, a book, a walk that goes nowhere.

## What I would tell my earlier self

Not much, because she would not have listened. But if I had to pick one thing, it would be this: the morning is not for getting ahead. It is for arriving. The work is still there at eight, and you are more use to it.

[^1]: I charge the phone in the kitchen, not the bedroom, and it stays in a drawer until I sit down at the desk. Willpower has very little to do with it. The drawer does the work.
