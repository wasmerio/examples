I have kept a notebook, on and off, since I was nineteen. For most of those years I kept it badly. I bought good paper, wrote a careful first page, and then let the thing sit in a bag until the cover bent. The problem was never discipline. The problem was that I thought the notebook was for keeping things.

## A place to put things down

Last autumn I changed one rule and the habit finally held: nothing in the notebook has to be worth keeping. It is a place to put things down so I can stop carrying them in my head. Half of what goes in is dull. Shopping lists share pages with sentences I want to steal from myself later, and I no longer mind the mix.

The pages fill faster this way, and I have stopped rereading them in the hope of finding something finished. What I find instead is the shape of a week: what I was worried about, what I noticed on the walk to the post office, which paragraph I rewrote four times before giving up.

## What actually goes in

Looking back through the last three, the entries fall into a few piles:

- Overheard sentences, written down fast, usually with the wrong punctuation.
- Titles for essays I will probably never write.
- Small facts I would otherwise look up twice. The train times, the name of the bakery, how much the printer charges per page.
- The first line of something, and nothing else.
- Lists of what I am reading, with a word or two beside each title.

None of these are the notes I imagined I would take when I was nineteen. They are more useful than those would have been.

## The rule about neatness

A friend who paints keeps her sketchbooks the same way and put it better than I can:

> The sketchbook is where I am allowed to be bad. If I am not bad in there, I will be bad in the painting.

I think about this whenever I catch myself writing slowly to keep the page pretty. Neat pages are a sign that I am performing for a reader who does not exist.[^1] The notebook has one reader, and she is forgiving.

So the rule now is simple. Write it down. Do not fix the spelling. Do not rip out pages. Date the top of each entry, because a date is the one thing you cannot reconstruct later. Everything else can be messy.

[^1]: The exception is the index I keep on the last page, which is neat because it has to work. Two lines per entry: a page number and a few words.
